What this is, from zero

No crypto background needed. Hashes, the one promise they make, why people try to break that promise on purpose, and the machine we built to help.

  1. 01A hash is a fingerprint

    Picture a magic blender. Put anything in (a password, a file, a whole Bitcoin transaction) and out comes one fingerprint made of letters and numbers.

    The same input always makes the same fingerprint. A different input should always make a different one. That is the whole promise, and the internet's trust leans on it.

  2. 02Big systems lean on it

    Huge systems pick one blender and trust it with everything that matters. Three of them, and the blender each one leans on:

    • Bitcoin SHA-256 Locks its entire transaction history in place.
    • Ethereum SHA3 / Keccak Runs through its smart contracts.
    • Solana, IPFS and more BLAKE3 A fast one, used by a lot of modern tools.

  3. 03A crack is called a collision

    If someone found a way to make the blender give two different inputs the same fingerprint, that is a collision: a crack in the foundation. A fake could then pass as the real thing.

    If it ever happened

    The worst case, way down the line:

    • Rewriting Bitcoin's history.
    • Forging Ethereum contracts.
    • Breaking the padlock icon on your bank's website.
    • Swapping a file for a fake that still "checks out".

    Where it really stands

    None of that has happened. Nobody has ever found a collision in the full versions of these three.

    That is exactly why people hunt for weaknesses on purpose, early and in public, before someone with bad intentions does.

  4. 04HashSmash is the public test

    HashSmash (opens in a new tab) is a real, public contest, backed by real cryptographers and judged by experts who helped design these hash functions. Anyone, human or AI, can enter.

    Entrants attack slightly weakened copies: the same blender with fewer rounds of scrambling. Public, scored, judged. Not theoretical.
